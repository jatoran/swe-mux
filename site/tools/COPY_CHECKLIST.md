# Copy checklist for swemux.dev

What to check before shipping words the site renders, and what has already been measured so it
is not re-litigated.

This sits in `tools/` beside the two files that hold the prose (`build.py`, `docs_content.py`)
because it governs them.
`tools/` is an input directory, not a page, so nothing here is served.
`site/README.md` section 5 states the site's voice rules; this file is the operational version
of them plus the machine-writing tells that section 5 does not cover.

Scope note: `site/index.html` had its own pass on 2026-08-28, which took the landing page from
3739 rendered words inside `<main>` to 2593, and removed every image caption.
What that pass learned is section 6.
`site/content/*.html` is still uncovered and is what a third pass is for.

---

## 1. The four cuts, in the order they find the most

Work in this order. Each one finds things the next one would miss.

1. **Copy written for the wrong audience.**
   A build instruction, a maintenance rule, or a design rationale rendered as visible text to a
   visitor.
   The example that prompted this checklist shipped on every generated page: "This page is
   generated by `site/tools/build.py`. Edit its source, not the HTML."
   If a contributor needs it, an HTML comment or `site/README.md` is where it goes.
2. **A claim with no number, no comparison, and no concrete detail.**
   Cut the claim rather than dressing it up.
   Superlatives are the usual carrier: "the single most confusing symptom", "the most important
   default", "cheap enough to run often".
   Either find the number or delete the sentence.
3. **Self-commentary tails.**
   A clause whose only job is to tell the reader the significance of the clause before it.
   "which is the whole mechanism", "and that is the point", "so the probe is not decoration",
   "which is what makes it worth building a layout at all".
   The fact is already on the page. Trust the reader to have it.
4. **Throat-clearing openers.**
   A sentence that announces the shape of the paragraph before the paragraph starts.
   "Two pairs of surfaces look like duplicates and are not, for the same reason each time."
   "There is a second rule stacked on top of that one, and it is what makes X safe:"
   Delete the announcement and start at the rule.

## 2. Banned outright

From `site/README.md` section 5 and the operator's voice profile, combined:

seamless, powerful, robust as a marketing adjective, effortless, unleash, supercharge,
revolutionize, game-changer, blazing fast, reimagined, unlock, leverage as a verb, delve,
holistic, nuanced, streamlined, optimized, "the key move", "what this enables",
"the future of", "in today's world", "at scale", "best practices", "foster", "drive innovation",
tapestry, landscape, ecosystem as a metaphor, realm, "testament to", "underscores the
importance", "it's worth noting that", "here's the thing", "at the heart of", "navigate
challenges", Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Ultimately, "plays a key role",
"serves as", "In other words".

**No em dash, ever.** Plain dash, comma, or full stop.
`build.py` fails the build if one reaches a page, so this one is enforced rather than
remembered.

## 3. Machine-writing tells

The first block is from the operator's voice profile and every item was found in one of his real
drafts.
The second block was added on 2026-08-28 from outside research, and each item was checked
against this corpus before being added.

**From the voice profile:**

- A joke or an "a.k.a." in a heading. He does not tell jokes; an LLM told to write "blunt"
  produces a comedian and it is wrong every time.
- Bold splattered several times per paragraph, including on list items needing no emphasis.
- Triadic ad copy. "No fluff. No extra steps. Just speed."
- Mock-comedic misery. "exports that make me want to walk into the ocean."
- The comedic ellipsis. "Models are... honestly pretty good these days."
- Reader-labels that do the reacting: "Reality check", "The good + the annoying", "TL;DR".
- Closing on "End." or "Yeah." as a manufactured deflation beat.
- Generic filler that could describe any project. "testable, debuggable, and maintainable."
- Deferential hedging. "I can see both perspectives, though perhaps..."

**Added 2026-08-28, with why each one earned its place:**

- **Negative parallelism, relentlessly.** "It's not X, it's Y" and "Not just A, but B".
  Named in the research as the structural habit that persists across model generations, because
  it organizes a contrast cleanly and therefore gets reached for every time.
  The tell is the relentlessness, not any single use.
  See section 4: on this corpus that hypothesis was **measured and rejected**, so check the
  distribution before acting on it.
- **Rhetorical questions used as transitions.** "Changing swe-mux rather than using it?"
  Fixed on the docs index; state it instead of asking it.
- **Structural predictability.** Every section the same length, every page the same shape, every
  list three items long.
  Human writing is lumpy: some sections are one sentence and some are a page.
- **Generic specificity.** An example that has the grammar of a detail but names nothing.
  "a large tree", "several installs", "a noisy day" are fine as bounds; they are not evidence.
- **Hedge stacking.** often, typically, generally, can be, may, tends to, in most cases.
  One hedge is a qualification. Three in a paragraph is an unwillingness to make a claim.
- **The stated moral.** Research phrasing: does the piece state its own moral out loud, or trust
  the reader to get it?
  This is section 1 item 3 seen from the outside, and it was the highest-yield cut on this
  corpus.
- **Intensifier-as-emphasis.** actually, genuinely, truly, really.
  Keep the ones marking a real contrast against a belief the reader holds ("whether the distro
  can *actually* reach the daemon"); cut the ones that are just volume.
- **Markdown residue in prose.** Bold as a substitute for sentence structure, and headings that
  are really list items.
  Named in the research as a training-data fingerprint.

Sources for the added block:
[Cherryleaf, indicators that suggest something was written by AI](https://www.cherryleaf.com/2026/02/indicators-that-suggest-something-was-written-by-ai/),
[Decrypt, the 5 biggest tells](https://decrypt.co/348923/5-biggest-tells-something-written-ai),
[The Last Fingerprint: how markdown training shapes LLM prose](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27006).
The third was read as a PDF and its extraction was partial; its em-dash and markdown-residue
findings corroborate the voice profile, and no number from it is relied on here.

## 4. Measured, and rejected. Do not redo this.

**The "rather than" antithesis is not this site's problem, despite looking like it.**

The raw count invites the opposite conclusion and one pass already reached it: 144 occurrences
across 31,000 rendered words, roughly one every 215 words, which reads as the site's dominant
sentence shape.

The distribution says otherwise. Over 669 rendered blocks of twelve words or more:

| Antitheses in the block | Blocks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| none | 539 | 81% |
| one | 116 | 17% |
| two or more | 14 | 2% |

The fourteen blocks carrying two were then read individually, and nearly every instance is
load-bearing: "arbitrated rather than fought over", "detected rather than configured", "unclear
rather than a guess", "announced rather than silent", "unmeasured rather than as zero".
Each names the wrong thing a reader would otherwise assume, which is precision and is exactly
what `site/README.md` section 5 asks for.

**So the rule is: cut the instance where the rejected alternative is a strawman nobody would
have proposed, and keep the rest.**
A blanket pass over this construction would flatten the prose and remove real distinctions.

Re-run the measurement rather than trusting this table if the corpus changes materially.
The scripts that produced it were throwaway; the method is: strip tags from the built pages,
split on block elements, count per block rather than per corpus.

## 5. The one editorial trap

The documentation is technical reference, and precision is not bloat.
A sentence carrying a real number, a real constraint, or a real failure mode stays even when it
is long.

**The test:** would removing it let a reader believe something false?
If yes, keep it.

Examples that stayed long on purpose, and why:

- The land queue's "no percentage is derived at either end, because this repository's own gate
  has steps that take 45 seconds and 3 seconds". Two real numbers and a real reason.
- The three-pot usage table's "No surface computes a total across them". Removing it would let a
  reader assume the figures add up.
- Every "no CI job on any host starts a daemon" sentence. That is a limitation stated before
  someone else finds it, which is the thing that buys credibility for the claims around it.

## 6. What the landing-page pass added, 2026-08-28

The four cuts in section 1 all applied, but a marketing page has three failure modes the
reference documentation does not, and each of these was worth more here than any of them.

**A caption is a confession.**
Fourteen images carried one, and ten were narration of what the picture already showed.
The rule that replaced them is absolute and is now in `site/README.md` section 8: the claim goes in
the section's prose, the image goes directly under the claim it is evidence for, and the frame holds
nothing else.
`alt` and `aria-label` stay and stay accurate; they are accessibility, not copy.
Before deleting one, check what it is carrying - four of the fourteen held a fact that existed
nowhere else on the page, and one of those was the **only** description of the land queue.
Rehome those; delete the rest.

**A placeholder is copy written for the wrong audience, and it is the worst case of it.**
Six dashed `.crop` boxes and two `.vis` diagram frames each drew a hatched rectangle holding a
paragraph describing a capture nobody had taken.
That paragraph is a brief for whoever takes the shot, rendered as visible text to a stranger, and
beside nine finished screenshots it reads as an unfinished page.
The specifications moved into `site/README.md` section 8, where the person who needs them is.

**A feature row is a claim, and the same test applies to it.**
On a page enumerating thirty-one of them, a row that names nothing a competitor lacks and no
boundary a cautious reader needs is filler even when every word in it is true.
Ten were cut on that test.
The test is not "is this true", it is "does a stranger's decision change if they read it".

Two things that pulled hard in the opposite direction and were right to:

- **A number in the copy that is visible in the screenshot under it.** Section 09's attention row
  says "four interrupts a day and two an hour" and the capture beneath it reads `3/4 interrupts
  today · 1/2 this hour`. That is the pairing working without a caption, and it is why the number
  went back in after a draft had cut it to "a hard daily budget".
- **Stated limits stay, and length is not the test.** The conceded edge on session survival, the
  three facts about what a Python install does not give you, and "no CI job anywhere starts the
  daemon" are all long and all stayed. Section 5's question is the one that decides it.

## 7. Before shipping

```
python site/tools/build.py            # regenerate, and commit the HTML with the source
node site/tools/check.mjs
python site/tools/contrast.py
python site/tools/check_changelog.py
```

A stale generated page beside a changed template has broken this gate more than once.
`build.py` also fails on an em dash and on an `/OWNER/` placeholder reaching a page.
